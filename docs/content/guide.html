What Airbrx is

One-liner: Airbrx gives your dashboards and AI agents a memory, so repeat questions stop running up your Snowflake and Databricks bills.

  • Where it sits: between a company’s apps (BI tools, AI agents, scripts) and its warehouse. When the same kind of question comes in again, Airbrx answers from memory instead of paying the warehouse to run it again.
  • Setup: one hostname change. Anything not in memory passes straight through to the warehouse.
  • Speed: cached answers come back about 10x faster.
  • Works with: Snowflake, Databricks and Postgres. Lead with Snowflake and Databricks.

Why now: when the idea started, it was a person in front of Tableau running about 400 queries a day. Today AI agents can send a million a day, often without anyone noticing.

Who to talk to

November is about companies already feeling warehouse cost pain. Abram’s sweet spot right now is finance and other teams with heavy reporting and data volume.

Good fits

  • Teams on Snowflake, Databricks or Postgres with dashboards or AI agents hitting the warehouse over and over.
  • Companies whose warehouse bill is growing as they add AI.
  • Anyone you know personally who runs data or analytics. Warm contacts first.

Not a fit today

  • Amazon Athena or BigQuery shops.
  • Streaming workloads. Airbrx is built for analytics.
  • Teams with no repeat dashboard or agent traffic.

Who decides: whoever writes the check, usually the CFO or VP of Finance. The engineers have to be comfortable too, and they often skip a second call because they’re already five projects behind. That’s why setup happens on the first call.

Four qualifying questions

  1. Are you on Snowflake, Databricks or Postgres?
  2. Do dashboards or AI agents send repeat traffic to it?
  3. Roughly what’s the monthly bill, and is it growing? (Ballpark is fine.)
  4. Who can change a hostname, and can they join a 30-minute call?

How to start

Don’t sell. Ask them to try it. Michael’s framing: “Could you do me a favor and just try it out?” With partners and consultants, ask how they could use it to make things better for their own clients. Nobody has to buy anything on the first call.

Frame it around growth, not waste. Ben has seen decision makers react badly when a pitch implies their team has been wasting money. Talk about keeping the bill under control as AI traffic grows.

Answers to common questions

Each answer below comes from Abram or Michael, except the Snowflake cache answer, which comes from Snowflake’s own documentation. If a question isn’t here, don’t guess: say you’ll check and bring Abram in.

They ask You answer
What if Airbrx goes down? It fails to pass-through: queries still reach your warehouse, you just stop getting the caching advantage. You can also point the hostname back at any time.
Will people see stale numbers? No. You control freshness: time limits, change detection, any rule you can write in SQL. Writes pass through and clear the affected cache.
Do you store our credentials? No. Your password or token passes through to the warehouse, like Tableau does.
Where does our data go? Cached results sit in Airbrx’s storage, encrypted and never inspected. Larger accounts can run Airbrx entirely in their own AWS.
Does it slow anything down? Only slightly, and only when Airbrx doesn’t have the answer yet: in Airbrx’s own testing, queries that weren’t in memory took about 1 ms longer. Answers that are in memory come back about 10x faster.
How hard is setup? One hostname change.
Can it stop runaway AI agents? Yes. It can block rogue agents and runaway queries, and it keeps a record of every query.
Doesn’t Snowflake already cache? Snowflake reuses only identical SQL, for 24 hours, and drops it when the data changes. Airbrx works across your tools and agents, with freshness rules you set.
Do you support BigQuery or Athena? Not today. Snowflake, Databricks and Postgres.
Can it run in the same region as our data? Yes. Airbrx can run its gateway in the same cloud region as your warehouse. That keeps traffic close to your data, avoids extra charges for moving data between regions, and keeps cached results in that region, which helps with data-residency rules. Larger accounts can also run Airbrx entirely in their own AWS.
Are you SOC 2 certified? SOC 2 Type II is expected by the end of 2026.
How do we see what we’d save? A free scan reads your query history and shows how your BI and AI tools use the warehouse. Abram can walk you through it.
How much does it cost? Pricing is being finalized. You can try it before you buy anything, and Abram will walk you through options on the hands-on call. Don’t quote prices, plan names or free periods.
