Summary

Every company starts on a free pilot, and small users stay free forever under a usage cap. Most customers pay a fixed monthly price, $299 to $1,999, stepped by what buyers can count: tenants, warehouses, users and features. Pro and Enterprise pay a platform fee plus a meter, because large accounts vary too much for one flat price. Savings show on the dashboard and statement as proof on every plan.

  • The pitch: memory for repeat queries in one gateway. A fixed, budgetable price for most teams, and a statement that shows what you got back.

  • What sets the plan: tenants, warehouses, users and features. Request counts and GB are fair use only.

  • Savings: shown in dollars beside the customer's own unit (Snowflake credits, Databricks DBUs) as the ROI and renewal story.

  • Launch offers: free pilots, a free-forever tier with a cap, and no FDE fees for small customers while Airbrx gets off the ground.

How savings are measured

On fixed plans, savings are proof, not the bill. Cost avoided is the warehouse compute that actually didn't run because of Airbrx, priced from the customer's own billing data. If a customer turns Airbrx off for a month, their bill should go up by about the amount we claimed.

  • Caching savings: queries answered from memory that would otherwise have kept the warehouse awake, woken it, or needed a bigger size or more clusters.

  • Outstanding Routing savings (only if routing is offered): misses sent to a smaller warehouse, plus wake-ups avoided on the large one.

  • Only bill-changing savings count. On uptime billing, a cached query on an already-awake warehouse saves little. The statement counts compute avoided, not the theoretical price of each query.

  • Prices come from the customer's data: Snowflake query attribution and metering history, Databricks billing usage and query history. Not list prices or averages.

  • Units: dollars first, with the customer's unit beside it (credits, DBUs; estimated compute hours for Postgres).

  • Outstanding Proof: a small random control group of repeat queries passes through, or a pause test the customer can request. Reconcile monthly; draft accuracy target within 10%.

Pilot, free tier and moving between plans

Every company pilots free. The pilot's savings report shows the return and recommends a plan by usage.

Pilot

  • Who: every company, any size. No charge, no card.

  • Length: 30 days by default. Extend to 60 or 90 days to help close, especially smaller companies. Abram or Amit approves.

  • Scope: one workload (a dashboard set, an app or an agent), every feature on, hosted by Airbrx. FDE routing setup included if routing is offered (assumption). Self-hosted pilots are an Enterprise conversation.

  • Start: the free scan captures a baseline from query history.

  • During: weekly savings snapshot; a check-in at the halfway point.

  • End: a savings report shows what Airbrx saved and recommends the plan that fits their tenants, warehouses and users. Choosing a plan keeps caching on with no gap.

  • No plan chosen: the account drops to the free tier. Caching stops above the free-tier usage cap; queries keep working and go straight to the warehouse.

Free tier

  • Free forever for startups and very small companies. It earns its keep through future growth, word of mouth and real usage data, and the cost to Airbrx is trivial.

  • Cap: a cached-request or tenant limit, so customers can "run everything you want, and we cap it if it gets out of control." Where the cap sits is open.

Moving between plans

  • Up: when the customer needs more tenants, warehouses, users or a higher-plan feature. We prompt in the app; the customer confirms.

  • Down: at a quarterly review if usage drops.

  • No surprise bills: above a plan's limits, additional requests go straight to the warehouse and the price doesn't change until the customer confirms a move.

Billing rules

Free through Business bill a fixed monthly price with no meter. Pro and Enterprise bill a platform fee plus a monthly meter.

  • Annual billing: prepay a year and pay for 10 months. Applies to fixed plans and to the Pro and Enterprise platform fee.

  • Meter (Pro, Enterprise): billed monthly in arrears from the statement the customer already sees. The basis is open (see Metering for large accounts).

  • Budget controls (Pro, Enterprise): a monthly cap on the share and spend alerts, so metered plans stay budgetable.

  • Outstanding Enterprise ceiling: a cap or annual true-up on the 20% share is open for negotiation on large contracts.

  • No overage on fixed plans: above plan limits, additional requests pass straight through to the warehouse uncached. Queries keep working, nothing extra is billed, and the price holds until the customer confirms a move up.

  • Never billed: cache misses, writes, pass-through traffic, request counts or GB. Those are fair use only.

  • Pricing page: shows the free pilot, the plan ladder (fixed prices for most teams, savings shown as proof) and "Talk to us."

Services, FDE and the free year

FDE and consulting are billed separately, except for small customers during launch.

  • Small customers (free tier, pilots, Team, Team Plus, Business): no FDE fees while Airbrx gets off the ground, including manual warehouse routing setup if routing is offered (assumption). This is a launch policy and can change later; say so up front.

  • Outstanding Manual routing (assumption): FDEs would configure warehouse routing by hand, free for small customers. Not confirmed.

    • Outstanding Automated routing: no confirmed intent to build it.

    • Outstanding Free FDE hour cap: draft, a few hours per customer. Covers setup and routing between warehouses the customer already has; a full warehouse redesign is a paid project.

  • Outstanding Pro and Enterprise: FDE billed at an FDE rate (to set). Open: include initial routing setup in the pilot to win the account.

  • Channel partners can deliver FDE work themselves or bill through Airbrx.

  • Free year: last resort only. Founder approval, a large deal that is stuck, written into that contract. Never in outreach or on the website.

  • Referrals: 15% of the customer's first-year subscription for a closed deal.

Why six plans, not two. Two prices are simpler to sell, but usage runs from a 10k/day dashboard to 1M/day agents. With two prices, most customers would pay far above or below the value they get. Risks and open decisions

Measurement gates billing only if the large-account meter is a savings share. Either way, the biggest risk is credibility: the savings statement has to match the real bill change, or the ROI story falls apart.

Risks

  • Business to Pro jump: $1,999 to $2,999 plus the meter. Pro must also sell on governance features (audit log, custom rules, SSO).

  • Thin savings at low volume: at 10k queries/day, caching alone saves little unless repeats are high. Routing (if offered) and the free tier would carry these customers.

  • Free FDE time: unpaid work that doesn't scale. Cap hours per customer and track payback as plan upgrades.

  • Ending launch offers: customers on free FDE or long pilots will expect them to continue. Say up front they are launch offers.

  • Audit log vs the pitch: the pitch promises a full audit log of AI queries; this plan keeps the full log on Pro. Adjust one or the other.

  • Storage cost on flat plans: absorbed in margin for now; a cache-size limit may be needed.
