1. Problem

Teams on Snowflake, Databricks and Postgres pay their warehouse to answer the same questions again and again, and AiRBRX cannot yet prove how much of that bill it removes. Without a trusted savings number, buyers will not pay, pricing cannot be set by value, and the Dec 17 pitch has no customer proof.

Who feels it

  • Buyers: dashboards (Power BI, Tableau) rerun the same work all day; AI agents send millions of repeat queries and keep warehouses awake 24/7. The CFO sees the bill climb faster than revenue.

  • AiRBRX: every current tenant runs under 10k requests/day, no tenant has a before-and-after baseline, and savings claims across materials range from 20% to 60%.

7. Pricing summary

Every company starts on a free pilot; most paid plans are fixed monthly prices stepped by tenants, warehouses, users and features; Pro and Enterprise add a meter. Savings show as proof. Internal draft for the founders; structure Medium confidence, numbers Low. Full detail: Airbrx Pricing Plan.

  • Plans: Free forever (usage cap, level open), Team $299, Team Plus $699, Business $1,999, Pro $2,999 + meter, Enterprise $8,999 + meter. Features step up by plan (tenants, warehouses, freshness rules, audit log and SSO on Pro, self-hosting and SLAs on Enterprise).

  • Pilot: free for any size, 30 days, extendable to 90 to close. No plan chosen means the free tier; queries keep passing through.

  • Moving between plans: move up when the customer needs more tenants, warehouses, users or a higher-plan feature; customer confirms.

  • Savings statement: four lines in dollars and credits or DBUs, testable against the real bill (draft target: within 10%).

  • Revised (Nov 3): following Michael and Ben, fixed plans are not tied to savings ("can feel like a trap"). Pro and Enterprise keep a meter; its basis is open (usage or 20% of cost avoided). Accurate savings metering is needed for billing only if the meter is a savings share.

  • Routing is an assumption, not a confirmed offer, manual or automated.

What pricing needs from the product: R2 to R4 (metering and statement), R9 and R10 (pilot and cap), R11 and R13 (plan engine and budget caps). Under the neutral alternative, R10 and R11 switch from savings to usage limits. Rejected pricing options are in section 9; open pricing items in section 12.

8. Solution

Price every cache miss as it runs, credit that cost on every later hit, and show the total as a statement line the customer can test. Around that meter sit the free scan (baseline), guided connect (first value), the pilot lifecycle (conversion) and the plan engine (expansion).

How savings are measured (proposed, to confirm with Michael)

  1. On a cache miss, log duration, warehouse ID, warehouse query ID and cache key.

  2. Cost of the miss = duration x warehouse rate (credits or DBUs per hour, from size and type, set at onboarding or read from the API). Snowflake: QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY. Databricks: system.billing.usage split by total_task_duration_ms.

  3. Every later hit on that key credits the same cost.

  4. Wake-ups avoided: when a hit lands while the warehouse is likely asleep, credit the minimum charge plus the idle tail before auto-suspend.

  5. Billable savings count only compute that changes the bill (uptime-billed warehouses must suspend more or downsize). Reconcile monthly against the actual bill.

User stories

Economic buyer (CFO, VP Finance)

  • As a CFO, I want to see my warehouse bill with and without AiRBRX in credits or DBUs, so I can check it against the invoice from Snowflake or Databricks.

  • As a CFO, I want a pilot that costs nothing and ends with a plan recommendation, so I can approve it without procurement.

  • As a Pro or Enterprise buyer, I want a monthly budget cap and spend alerts, so the meter never surprises me.

Technical owner (Head of Data, platform lead)

  • As a Head of Data, I want to run a read-only scan that returns totals only, so I can estimate savings without signing up or exposing query text.

  • As a platform lead, I want to connect my own warehouse and point one tool at the gateway in under 30 minutes, so setup happens on the first call.

  • As a platform lead, I want to audit any savings figure down to the query, time of each hit and estimate method, so I can defend the invoice.

Champion (agent builder, analytics engineer)

  • As an agent builder, I want a code sample for my tool after picking it from a list, so I can swap the hostname without reading docs.

  • As an agent builder, I want an alert when my agent's traffic stops or spikes, so I find outages and runaway agents before the bill does.

AiRBRX team (Abram, Amit, FDE)

  • As Abram, I want to see every pilot's days left, savings to date and recommended plan, so I know who to call this week.

  • As Amit, I want to extend a pilot to 60 or 90 days with one action and a logged reason, so extensions stay controlled.

  • As Adam, I want funnel events from signup to first cache hit, so I can find where onboarding loses people.

Edge cases: traffic that bypasses the gateway; Postgres with no per-query bill; a warehouse the gateway cannot see; pilot ends mid-month; customer turns AiRBRX off; statement disputes.
